How to read it

Breadth: <30 = weak participation, 30–70 = mixed, >70 = broad. Extremes can persist and are not automatic reversal signals.

Volume Activity: 100 = normal relative volume. 130 means aggregate activity is roughly 30% above normal after per-stock normalisation and capping.

Directional Balance: positive = more relative-volume weight on rising stocks; negative = more on falling stocks; 0 = balanced weights or unchanged prices. If all valid stocks have zero recorded volume, balance is undefined. This is not money flow.

High-volume breadth: percentage of stocks with a valid volume comparison trading at least 1.5× their own prior-20-observed-session average volume.

Understanding volume participation

Each view compares a stock's recorded daily volume with its own usual volume, then combines the valid comparisons across stocks.

Relative volume (RVOL) = today's volume ÷ average volume of the prior 20 observed trading bars. Today is excluded from the average. RVOL 2 means twice the stock's usual volume.

Activity

How busy is trading compared with normal? The index averages each valid stock's RVOL, capped at 5× so one extreme observation does not dominate, then multiplies by 100.

100 = normal average activity; 200 = twice normal on average. It does not measure total rupiah traded.

Directional balance

Is relative volume concentrated more in rising or falling stocks? Each valid stock's RVOL, capped at 5×, is given a positive sign when its close rises and a negative sign when it falls, compared with its previous observed close.

+100 = all relative-volume weight is on rising stocks; −100 = all is on falling stocks; 0 = the weights cancel or prices are unchanged. Unchanged stocks still count in the total weight. This is not money flowing into or out of the market.

If every valid stock has recorded volume of zero, there is no volume weight to divide by and balance is unavailable. That is different from a balance of zero.

High-volume breadth

What share of stocks are unusually active? This is the percentage of stocks with a valid volume comparison whose RVOL is at least 1.5×.

40% means 40 out of every 100 valid stocks traded at least 50% above their usual volume.

A valid volume comparison needs recorded volume, a positive historical baseline and usable price and indicator data. Missing observations are excluded and stay missing; they are never changed to zero. For Directional balance, SMA uses the last N available balance observations; EMA uses N as its smoothing span and retains its prior valid state across gaps. Undefined dates stay blank, and smoothing resumes on the next available observation.

Understanding Coverage

Coverage tells you how much of the expected stock universe has usable indicator data on a particular date. It is a data-completeness measure, not a measure of how many stocks are bullish.

Target stocks (warmed up)
Stocks in the retrieved roster that have enough history to calculate the indicators: at least 350 observed trading bars. This is the meaning of “warmed”. Observed bars are recorded stock sessions, not calendar days. A newly listed stock is excluded until it has enough history.
Valid stocks
Target stocks with usable data for all three breadth indicators: MACD, MA100 and Supertrend on that date. A target stock with missing or unusable indicator data is not valid for that date.

Example: 900 target stocks are warmed up, and 810 have valid indicators today. Coverage = 810 ÷ 900 × 100 = 90%. The other 90 lack usable indicator data; it does not mean their prices fell.

A downward spike means fewer stocks had usable observations on that date. A short drop followed by recovery can look like a vertical line when many years are shown together. Zoom in and hover to see the date and stock counts.

Below 95% is a data-quality warning. If no target stocks have usable indicators, the whole date is excluded instead of publishing a misleading 0%.

Historical targets use today's retrieved stock roster, rather than a certified historical constituent list. Stocks no longer in that roster may be absent from earlier history, so survivorship bias remains.
